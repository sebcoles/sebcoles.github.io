Nothing is certain except death, taxes, and agency (in humans).

My #3 coaching point for junior managers is on the topic of agency. Agency is the capacity to make your own choices; it is the variable between a report (the agent) doing what the manager (the principal) wants.

As a manager, your success is dependent on guiding the beautifully unique and volatile people around you in the direction you want. The way we achieve this is through alignment. If you go in the direction I ask, there is something in it for you (money, titles, status, mastery, etc.).

However, there is a catch. At a junior management level, most of your reports do not know what they want, and they will be mired in deeply held beliefs about what they should want.

A huge part of a people manager's role is to create rapport and safety so that an honest conversation can happen about what a report really wants. That can take time, it takes vulnerability, and can involve some hard conversations that your reports may never have had before.

But it is worth it, because when you get true alignment, motivation becomes intrinsic. People perform for themselves, for their mission, for their family. Managing people with clear direction and motivation stops feeling like management at all; it feels like partnership.

Managing people when there is not alignment feels like drag; it feels like pushing. The answer to that is going back to alignment.

Management is not about tricks and tools to manipulate people or force momentum. It is about finding alignment between a report and the company's direction, and then getting out of their way.
