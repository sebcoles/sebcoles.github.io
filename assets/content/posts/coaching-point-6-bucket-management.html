Career friendships are important to success.

Coaching point #6 for junior managers is the concept of bucket management.

The bucket is your energy in a given day. When the bucket is empty, your patience, empathy and motivation are gone, and we need to avoid that. It is unsustainable, impacts your health, and is a path to burnout.

However, as a new manager, you hit two problems. The first is that you encounter new, bucket-expensive activities such as conflict resolution or shit-shielding. When you are a new manager and unpractised, they take a lot of energy. The second is your new ability to fill other people's buckets with your own, whether that is being a shoulder to cry on or a cheerleader. You are burning the bucket from both ends.

As a junior manager, you need to find avenues to fill the bucket until you have learned to use less water. Some of this is hobbies, rest, family, and exercise, of course. But it is also career friendships, where check-ins and catch-ups give us a net increase in the bucket, and can also be productive.

Group therapy sessions with peers to moan and share gallows humour are actually healthy and build relationships. Keeping in touch with old colleagues who can safely challenge you gives you space to reflect. Having external coaches and conversations with people partners are all net bucket.

You do not need permission to do this. It is not wasted time. It is actually vital to succeeding.

Management is like working out a new muscle. It hurts to start with if you are doing it right, but gets easier with practice. The trick is to accept that you are going to spend more energy while you are learning, and deliberately build ways to refill the bucket until you do.
