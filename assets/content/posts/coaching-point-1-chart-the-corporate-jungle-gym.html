Accept your role is to chart the corporate jungle gym.

This is my #1 coaching point for junior managers. Every organisation is a jungle gym of people, process, and tech. Some of the equipment is OK, some is broken, some is out of date and no longer used, some requires maintenance, some is being replaced, some of it was never finished, and some of it never made sense in the first place.

Every org has founding Dave's monkey bars, no longer suitable but adhered to like religion. Lifer Jane's amazing slide (if you are the right body type). It is possible they may have new Exec Andrew, who wants to build a new jungle gym that looks like the last one they played with and refuses to acknowledge the current gym exists. And there is always newbie Sally, trying to implement a very sensible safety mat, but has not got the influence to deliver it yet.

A manager's role is to chart a path through this jungle gym. That might mean building or buying new equipment, performing maintenance, jumping hoops, or partnering with people from different gym areas to deliver improvements, so that your team can traverse. It may even mean bypassing equipment, or breaking something. That is your judgement as a manager.

Getting shit done is not someone with a bulldozer, it is intelligence and judgement. Charting the most effective path. Navigating tech, process, and people (the last one being the hardest).

Managers that perceive the above as blockers and inertia are the ones who burn out. The ones who accept their navigational role and develop the skills to deal with socio-technical systems are the ones who deliver, win, and have fun.
