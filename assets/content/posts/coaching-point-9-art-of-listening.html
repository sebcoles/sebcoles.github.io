Being a good listener has little to do with your ability to take in information.

Junior Management Tip #9 is the art of listening, or better, the ability to help someone feel heard. Feeling heard is an emotional experience. Walking away from a conversation feeling the other person "gets it", they "understand", they know how I feel. Achieving that experience has less to do with information processing, retention, or problem-solving, and more to do with how you make someone feel.

The skills you need are emotional labelling. Adults suck at just saying how they feel if something made them angry, upset or sad. But it's totally OK, and encouraged, to label other people's emotions, as long as it's non-judgemental. A few "gosh, did that leave you feeling X?" or "I think I would have felt Y in that scenario" is all it takes. You're not just hearing words, but also the feelings behind them.

Skill 2 goes back to questioning. Questions that show your vulnerability. "Can you tell me a bit more about X?" is probably my favourite catchphrase. Questions are evidence of listening.

The last skill goes by different names, whether it's mirroring or reflecting, and Chris Voss's book Never Split the Difference covers this in depth. My previous techniques are signals, but someone needs to be receptive to them for them to land, and that requires some degree of rapport. One of the quickest ways to build rapport with someone in full rant mode is to simply repeat the last word of a sentence as a question, matching the energy levels, tone and style of the person you're talking with. It can make you sound like part of the tribe. That's a proper dark art 😬 so only use it for the greater good.

That's it. Label feelings, ask questions and reflect the last word as a question in a similar style. People will walk away feeling heard, which they probably wanted more than any sort of action.
