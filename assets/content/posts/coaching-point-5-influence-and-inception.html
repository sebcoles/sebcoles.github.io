Inception, or at least creating the circumstances where someone can arrive at an idea themselves, is one of the most powerful tools of a manager.

Coaching point #5 for junior managers is an introduction to influence. You will come across metaphorical rocks that you can move, and sometimes you need to convince the rock to move itself. There are two key parts to achieving this. The first can be learned and practiced, the second requires maturity.

Skill 1 is being able to ask safe but targeted questions. Great questions force reflection. They make someone look inside their own thinking and expose vulnerabilities in their logic. But safe questions are not challenges, they are curious. Safe questions show your own vulnerability and a desire to understand.

Instead of "Why did you?", try "Tell me more about this?" Instead of "Did you think about?", try "What happens when this?"

The questions then need to be targeted around the area of concern. If there is a weakness in the logic, ask questions around it and start to paint some of the picture. The key is to make sure they finish the picture though. That is when the insight happens. Do not give them the answers. Keep asking questions.

This takes patience and practice, and a lot of reflection to get good at.

The second is simply keeping your ego in check. There is no great reveal that it was you all along, and you will not get thanked in the short term. In fact, they may genuinely believe they arrived at the idea all by themselves, which is the point and will bring confidence with it. The goal is not to prove that you were right; it is to help someone see something for themselves.

The burden of management, I am afraid.
