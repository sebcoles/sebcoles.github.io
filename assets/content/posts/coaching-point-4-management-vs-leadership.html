Management is the art of maintenance, leadership is the art of change.

Coaching point #4 for managers is the distinction between managing and leading, why both are equally important, and both are learnable.

Taking a car as an example. Management fills the car with petrol. Management makes sure its MOT is valid. Management drives the car, and good management checks seatbelts. Management is getting from A to B. Management is scheduling, planning, resource allocation, finances, projects, compliance, incidents, suppliers, product, people. Management is delivery.

Leadership is getting people in the car in the first place. Leadership is communicating why B is the place to go, why it is safe and what is in it for you. Leadership is saying let us get a different car, and nudging the organisation to agree. Leadership is saying let us get a boat, and getting most onboard. It is changing the environment and getting people to follow you in taking the risk.

Totally different skill sets. Both required. Management without leadership is a slow, painful path to inertia and irrelevance. Leadership without management is just hot air.

One is structured, but requires context switching, dealing with load, distractions, maintaining focus, interactions, thinking in systems and processes, scale.

The other is rooted in psychology, and deals with the far more unpredictable and unique nature of gooey humans and their feelings. It takes clear communications, listening, influence.

Both have extensive fields of study and require intentional practice to get good at. I am afraid you need to put down the technical book of your craft and pick up Your Brain at Work. Read, experiment, reflect, and read some more, and you can develop these skills too.
