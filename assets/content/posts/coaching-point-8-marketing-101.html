There is no such thing as overcommunication. Only undercommunication.

Junior management point #8 is Marketing 101. 1) Know your customer 2) The marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion).

You have multiple customers. Your reports, your management, your peers and, for me, the colleague base we are trying to protect. Knowing them means understanding what frustrates them (pains), what matters to them (gains), and how they talk about it. Only then can you apply the marketing mix.

Product – As a manager, you need to learn how to talk about your team's work in terms of what matters to your customer. The benefits are what you're selling - pain relief or rocket fuel. Technical details mean little if your customer doesn't care about them, no matter how cool they are.

Price – Everything has a cost, even internally. Is it a 30-minute chat? Training or migration time? A PBI? A sprint? What friction are you asking your customer to accept, and is the benefit worth the cost?

Place – How does your customer want to access your product? A wiki? A portal? A library? Miro? Not you. Not your team. Your customer. I've seen this own goal far too many times. 🤦‍♂️

Promotion – The easiest, yet most neglected. Promoting an initiative, platform or product isn't a Slack message and three minutes at an all-hands. It's a continual effort to get the message into the channels your customers actually use. Those may be channels you don't use yourself, and you may need to repeat the message weekly for a quarter.

That's it. A little basic marketing upskilling can be super effective for managers. Dark arts.
