Politics is an integral part of management.

Coaching point #2 for junior managers is to break down the stigma of politics and understand its role in a prioritisation system that has no formula.

It took me many years to get over politics, and many discussions with my mentors on this topic. My turning point came when I read about a political theory called agonism - the idea that conflict between respected opponents is a productive way to prioritise resources between competing ideas that are difficult to compare.

Agonism means the struggle, which sounds like a much more apt way of describing the experience of advocating your position at the corporate table.

Agonism only works when all players struggle to the best of their ability. The struggle forces us to prioritise our own asks, to barter, to discard, to push, and to challenge others.

I willingly engage in the struggle. It is one of the hardest parts of the role, but having the right attitude and respect for the process has stopped it from burning more energy than it needs to.

Politics is not manipulation or dysfunction. It is simply how we make decisions when there is not a formula for deciding the best use of resources.
